# Scroll Hole: content strategy

## The point of the game
**Scrolling feels like watching. Your memory says otherwise.** The visitor scrolls with no clock and no goal, sees themselves doing it, then is quizzed on what they just "watched". The score is their own, measured seconds after they felt entertained.

## The story
| # | Screen | What the visitor does | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Start | points a finger | "Scroll as long as you like." |
| 2 | Scroll | flicks, pinches | reels with a handle and a caption each |
| 3 | Mirror | watches | "This is you." (their face, sped up) |
| 4 | Warning | none | "Spot the real frame. 5 reels. 4 screenshots each. Only one is real." |
| 5 | Five questions | clicks the screenshot they think is real | four near-identical screenshots of one reel they saw; then right/wrong, what each fake changed, and the real reel playing on the right |
| 6 | Result | reads, then presses Continue | "3 / 5 Retained" · "You watched 11 reels in 17 s. You spotted the real frame 3 of 5 times." · one verdict line |

## Voice rules
- Plain spoken English, as few words as still make sense. Say what happened; never blame the visitor.
- Every number names what it measures.
- Every question is about a reel that visitor actually saw, and the real frame is exactly what they saw, so it is always fair.
- No claim about what "most people" score. The only comparison shown is this booth's own average, after 10 players.

## Honest limits
- The idea that "almost everyone scores under 20%" is an assumption, not a finding. The game reports each visitor's real result and the booth's real average.
- Quiz difficulty depends on how long they scrolled. A short scroll gives an easier quiz.
- Reels are short illustrated loops, not real creator videos, so "retention" here is of their visuals. Each wrong answer makes one big change from a different kind (sky, clothes, crowd, scenery), so they are easy to tell apart. Some are still easier than others: a night sky is easier to spot than different clothes.

## The closing film (spoken, with captions)
"You already know this." / "You have read the warnings. You have seen your screen time report. You know your number." / "And still, the phone is in your hand." / "Knowing was never the problem." / "The hard part is the moment you reach for it. Notice that moment."
Rules: describe what happens, never blame; the warning is worded "linked to" (the research is correlational), and nothing in the film claims a cure or a statistic.
